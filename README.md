# SNHU-Travel-Agile-Project
Agile project planning for SNHU Travel, covering Scrum practices, user stories, sprint reviews, and continuous improvement.
CS 250: Software Development Lifecycle

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective for the SNHU Travel project. This project brought together the Agile and Scrum practices I applied throughout CS 250, including user stories, sprint planning, testing, communication, and continuous improvement.

How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by understanding what users want to accomplish, asking questions, and translating their expectations into specific requirements. User stories help organize those requirements by describing who needs a feature, what they need, and why it matters. Adding acceptance criteria makes the expected behavior clear and provides a way to test whether the implementation meets the user's needs.

How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach development by breaking a larger problem into smaller tasks, building working functionality, and testing as I go. This course reinforced the importance of gathering feedback throughout development instead of waiting until the program is finished. In my future work, I hope to use a prioritized product backlog, short development cycles, sprint reviews, and retrospectives to stay focused on user needs and improve my process.

What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member means communicating clearly, completing agreed-upon work, and taking responsibility for the quality of my contributions. It also means raising obstacles early, listening to feedback, and helping teammates solve problems. A successful team shares knowledge and works toward a common goal while remaining willing to adjust its approach.
